Tousik Samui

Physicist (PhD, TIFR) · Data, machine learning and scientific computing · Postdoctoral Fellow, IMSc Chennai

I work with large, noisy datasets, statistics and code. For eight years I have built Monte Carlo simulations, machine-learning classifiers and analysis pipelines in C++ and Python for particle-physics research, and I ran a 440-core HPC cluster for a university department. I also wrote DynamicR, an open-source package that is now part of FastJet Contrib.

More about me

I am from West Bengal. I studied physics at Ramakrishna Mission Residential College, Narendrapur (University of Calcutta), and at IIT Kanpur. I did my PhD at the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR), Mumbai, on signals of extra dimensions at particle colliders. For that work I wrote my first Monte Carlo event generator from scratch.

Since 2018 I have been a postdoctoral fellow at HRI Prayagraj, IISER Kolkata and, since 2024, IMSc Chennai. My research is on collider phenomenology: jets and jet substructure, physics beyond the Standard Model, and new analysis methods for the LHC and future colliders. Most of this work is a data problem at heart: simulate millions of events, design good features, separate a rare signal from a large background, and check with statistics that the result is real. I have published 18 peer-reviewed papers (10 as corresponding author) and given about 40 talks, including an invited talk to the CMS B2G group at CERN.

Alongside research I have always looked after computing. At IISER Kolkata I ran the department's HPC cluster and designed its website and live job dashboard, and earlier I built a similar status page for the HRI cluster. I enjoy teaching too: I have lectured on data analysis with ROOT, on quantum field theory, and on group theory.

I am now looking for my next role, and I am happy to move into a new field. Data science, machine learning, quantitative research and HPC or research software engineering all use the same skills I use every day. I am open to positions in India or abroad, in industry or in academia.

DynamicR: open-source C++ package

The official implementation of the Dynamic Radius jet algorithm, a clustering method in which each cluster's size adapts to the local data. Released in FastJet Contrib, the standard add-on library for jet clustering, with documentation, an example and a regression test.

  • C++
  • Algorithm design
  • Open source
  • Testing
GitHub · FastJet Contrib · Paper

Collider data analysis with CMS Open Data

Applied the Dynamic Radius method to public CMS Open Data in a search for new heavy particles, and showed a clear gain in sensitivity. The CMS B2G group invited me to present the method in 2026.
