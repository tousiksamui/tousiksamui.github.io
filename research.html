Research

High energy physics phenomenology

The Standard Model of particle physics works remarkably well, but it does not explain dark matter, neutrino masses, the matter–antimatter asymmetry of the universe, or the pattern of quark and lepton flavours. My research asks how answers to these questions could show up at high-energy colliders, and how we can see them in real data.

I work across the whole chain of a collider study: building a model, generating Monte Carlo events, simulating the detector, designing the analysis, and interpreting the result with statistics. When the tools I need do not exist, I write them.

Jets and jet substructure

At the LHC, most interesting final states contain jets: sprays of hadrons from quarks and gluons, or from fast-moving heavy particles such as W, Z, Higgs bosons and top quarks. Standard jet algorithms use a fixed jet radius. With collaborators I proposed the Dynamic Radius jet clustering algorithm, in which the radius of each jet follows its own radiation pattern during clustering (JHEP 2023). We have since generalised it for more robust analyses (arXiv:2609.29862), and used it with public CMS Open Data to improve searches for vector-like top partners (Phys. Rev. D 2026).

Other substructure studies include quark–gluon discrimination in heavy dijet resonance searches (EPJC 2021), the polarisation of boosted W bosons with substructure and multivariate analysis (EPJC 2023), leptoquark models probed through top polarisation (JHEP 2025), and left- versus right-handed couplings of heavy neutrinos (PRD 2025).

Quantum observables and precision at future colliders

Recent measurements have shown quantum entanglement between particles produced at colliders. I am interested in hadronic decays, which are more common but harder to use. We developed an unfolding method to recover entanglement observables in Higgs decays to jets and leptons at a muon collider (arXiv:2512.09121). For the FCC-ee, I am working on Higgs production in lepton–photon collisions, including a Monte Carlo event generator with lepton and photon distribution functions (arXiv:2603.27822).

Physics beyond the Standard Model

In my PhD I studied collider signals of extra dimensions: mixing between the Higgs boson and the radion in warped models (NPB 2017), and missing-energy signals of large extra dimensions, for which I wrote a dedicated event generator. Later work covers dark matter (PRD 2019, EPJC 2024), a neutrinophilic U(1) model (PRD 2021), the Type-X two-Higgs-doublet model (PRD 2023, PLB 2024), leptoquarks and vector-like leptons (PRD 2023, NPB 2024), and lepton flavour violation at electron–proton colliders (PLB 2026).

Software

  • DynamicR (C++): the official implementation of the Dynamic Radius jet algorithm, released in FastJet Contrib (v1.104 onwards). I wrote and maintain it. Code on GitHub.
  • Dynamic Radius animation (Python): draws each step of the clustering as a video, for talks, teaching and debugging.
  • Monte Carlo event generators: for Kaluza–Klein graviton towers (PhD) and for lepton–photon initiated processes at the FCC-ee.
  • Pythia8 extension (C++, in progress): modified colour connections for heavy, long-lived coloured states.
  • HPC cluster dashboards (PHP/HTML): live job-status pages for the HRI cluster and the IISER Kolkata Kepler cluster.
